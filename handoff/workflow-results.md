## review:logo-scroll:brief-fit
**Verdict:** All three answer the ticket (dock, tile motion, paw feedback, reduced motion), and they work in structurally different ways: A is one scroll-linked element that docks into the header, B splits the logo into tiles, C keeps the hero static and fades a compact logo into the header. A's main dock and all of C are calm, smooth and good enough for a product owner to choose from. B is not. Its core idea doesn't read on screen: the tiles drift apart mostly while they are already hidden behind the opaque header, so the viewer sees a faded grey wordmark, then tile shards, then an empty header. A's fallback path also leaves a large blank hole at the top of the hero. The other findings are minor. The integrator report (report.md) did not exist when I reviewed. I made the extra probe images in scratchpad/protos/logo-scroll/review2 from the dev server that was already running on port 3109 (I didn't start or stop any process) and edited no code.
- **major** [B] The tile cascade can't be read and looks like the logo is breaking. The tiles drift up into the stair-step shape mostly while the header is covering them, the wordmark becomes a grey ghost, and for about 50px of scroll there is no whole logo anywhere. The 'click back together' happens in a different element, with nothing on screen linking it to the hero tiles.
  - evidence: review2/B-phone-strip.png and review2/B-80-130.png (390px wide): at 80px the tiles are offset vertically and the wordmark is about 20% grey. At 110 and 130px only a 2px red sliver and parts of the green and blue tiles show under the header, and the header slot is empty. The header logo first appears at about 160px. Desktop shows the same in review2/B-desk-strip.png (wordmark grey at 100px, tiles cut off with an empty header at 200px), and frames 4-5 of shots/video-B.webm match. Cause: tile-cascade.module.css L204-220 moves the tiles mostly upward (-28%, -20%, -12%) and only ±6% sideways. L133 and L147-166 run the drift on the 'exit' range with the header height as inset, so it plays out under the opaque header. tile-cascade-behaviour.ts sets DOCK_RATIO = 0.35, so the header logo waits until 65% of the hero logo is covered.
  - fix: Run the drift while the logo is still fully visible (drop the header inset, or move the range earlier, e.g. 'cover 45%' to 'cover 65%'). Make the movement mostly sideways (about ±5% X, at most 4% Y). Keep the wordmark fully opaque until the tiles regroup. Raise DOCK_RATIO to about 0.6-0.7 so the header tiles have joined up before the hero tiles go under the header, so no frame is without a logo.
- **major** [A] In browsers without scroll-driven animations (iOS 16-18, Firefox 158 and older), the logo jumps into the header after only 8px of scroll and leaves a blank band about 220px tall above the headline. The band stays until about 200px of scroll.
  - evidence: review2/fb-A-strip.png, made by turning off CSS.supports('animation-timeline') and the scroll-driven animation on the moving logo, which is exactly the .root[data-io] path in dock.module.css L202-214. At 0px the hero is normal. At 20px the logo is docked and the hero shows empty space from y≈64 to y≈290 before the eyebrow text. At 60px and 120px the blank band is still there. Cause: the moving logo is position: sticky, so its original 201px box stays reserved, and the sentinel is only 0.5rem tall (dock.module.css L159-165, dock-fallback.tsx).
  - fix: In the fallback, don't make the logo sticky. Let the hero logo scroll away normally and fade or slide the compact header logo in once the hero is about 80% under the header, using the same IntersectionObserver pattern as C. Alternatively, place the sentinel at the hero logo's bottom edge so the hole is already off screen when it docks.
- **minor** [A] In reduced motion, the header logo's fade is tied to scroll position, so if the reader stops mid-scroll the brand stays half-transparent and its colours look pastel, right next to the half-hidden hero logo.
  - evidence: shots/A-rm-150.png. Pixel samples of the header logo tiles: (238,194,191), (187,229,183), (197,201,243), against #CB2322, #01AD01 and #263FF1. Cause: dock.module.css L252-258 (fade-in on scroll(root) between --fade-start and --fade-end). B and C avoid this by using a timed, on/off fade.
  - fix: Use a timed 150-200ms opacity transition switched by an IntersectionObserver for reduced motion in every browser, as B and C do, so the logo is never left half-faded.
- **minor** [All] The docked header logo is too small for its wordmark to be read, and all three variants end in this same docked state, so none of them explores the trade-off. A product owner will notice that 'Friends of Animals' turns into a grey smudge.

  - fix: Raise this as a decision in the report and the PR (#13/#14): either use a taller phone header slot, or accept that the tiles alone are what people recognise at header size. Changing the lockup itself needs the brand owner, because the logo can't be altered.
- **minor** [All] A stand-in artefact adds a line that isn't in the artwork: a thin green line across the join between the paw pad and the hands, visible at rest and more obvious as a slanted line when A or B tilts the paw.

  - fix: Overlap the two bands by 1-2 units, or clip the mark as one ellipse, since the variants animate the mark as a whole anyway. The real SVG from #6 removes the problem.
- **minor** [C] The paw bounce uses an uneven scale, which briefly squashes the mark out of shape. The ticket and the brief's done checklist say the logo's proportions never change.
  - evidence: paw-lead.module.css L195, L218 and L240: transform: translateY(0) scale(1.025, 0.97) in paw-bounce, paw-bounce-1 and paw-bounce-2.
  - fix: Use a lift on translateY only, or an even scale (e.g. 1.03), for the landing beat.

  - fix: In report.md and on the sheets, lead each variant with its in-motion frames (e.g. 80px and 130px) or with the video. Correct the B comment. Note that A's left alignment comes from how its dock works, not from a separate layout choice.
